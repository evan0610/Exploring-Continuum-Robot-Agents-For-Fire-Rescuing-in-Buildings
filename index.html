Exploring Continuum Robot Agents For Fire Rescuing in Buildings

A flexible robot brings observation and local action into building rescue blind zones.

Steer toward a target. Deliver water through the body. Switch to material transport. Explore the task sequence in three miniature building environments.

01 / Research question

How can a robot act where access is restricted?

Dense buildings contain narrow openings and occluded interiors. Inspired by endoscopic exploration, this project combines a steerable continuum body with robot-view sensing and working channels. The aim is to bring inspection and local intervention closer to targets that are difficult to reach.

    03 / Proposed system

    Connect a local observation to a robot action.

    The proposed architecture connects image understanding to continuum action through visual feedback. It specifies how observations could guide targeting, tip steering, and tool operation. The videos document physical task behavior; they do not independently establish an end-to-end autonomous implementation of this architecture.

    Image understanding

    Interpret the scene.

    Identify the task target and relevant obstacles from the robot view. The design also considers spatial reasoning and context from previous observations.

    Continuum action

    Coordinate movement and tools.

    Connect the task to bending, advancement, spray direction, and ordered material transport. A new observation informs the next action.

    Research foundations: RoboBrain 2.0 and EndoVLA

    RoboBrain 2.0 motivates the understanding layer. EndoVLA motivates visual instruction and action for a flexible instrument. These are related research foundations.

    04 / Robot embodiment

    A flexible body with a steerable working tip.

    The front end is designed to bend in any direction around its axis. The body carries working pathways to the tip, allowing observation and intervention to share one platform.

    01 Visual imaging
    Robot-view sensing for target localization.
    02 Illumination
    Light near the tip for close-range inspection.
    03 Liquid delivery

    Indoor access and exterior aiming.

    06 / Current scope

    What is shown, and what remains to be evaluated.

    Recorded prototype tasks

    Camera views and physical task behavior in three building mock-ups: target-oriented tip motion, water spraying, and material transport. The demonstration targets represent fire-related tasks; they are not evidence of live-fire suppression.

    Evaluation still required

    Controlled trials, autonomy / operator-intervention reporting, heat and smoke robustness, and field deployment. Mask transport does not establish oxygen delivery or medical benefit. Training and robot-control code are not released here.
